How Chicago Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling every year in hard-water areas.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs such as discolored hot water, popping and rumbling, dampness under the tank, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number on your phone now so you can reach us fast the moment something seems off. A quick call saves time, money, and cold showers.
