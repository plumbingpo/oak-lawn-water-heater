Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Cicero

Many premature tank failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the manufacture date, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that point and also shows rusty water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement after a leak.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a surprise cleanup.

Common Water Heater Problems in Cicero

Water heaters in and around Cicero face a familiar set of stresses. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, minerals can be a real factor: calcium and magnesium settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, insulating the water from the burner, which produces the rumbling and popping you may hear during a heating cycle. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, which is why annual descaling is worth keeping up with in hard-water areas.

Then there's age. When the sacrificial anode rod is gone, usually somewhere in the second half of a tank's life, the tank starts to rust from the inside, and a drip from the bottom seam is usually the first visible sign. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, gas tanks in some houses and electric tanks in others, so our technicians handle everything from failed thermocouples and draft problems to burned-out elements and failed thermostats.

Well water can bring iron, sulfur, and grit into the tank, and a sulfur smell in the hot water usually points to a reaction with the anode rod that a different rod can fix. Older installations often need code updates when a heater is replaced: an expansion tank on closed systems, a correctly piped T&P discharge line, drain pans where a leak would hit finished floors, and earthquake straps in areas that require them. Every one of these problems can be fixed, and most are much cheaper to address early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a diagnostic visit from our team can keep a small problem from turning into a flooded floor. Give us a call at (708) 230-1529 to set up a visit.
