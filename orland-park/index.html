How Orland Park Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year in hard-water areas.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms such as discolored hot water, rumbling during heating, dampness under the tank, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a flooded utility room.
